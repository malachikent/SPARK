hypochlorite. The oxygen of borneol attacked the sodium hypochlorite, leaving chlorine
attached to the alcohol. Once the sodium bicarbonate and sodium bisulfite were added, the
chlorine was removed and the carbonyl group was formed, regarding the ketone. Refluxing the
solution expedited the exothermic reaction. The process of liquid-liquid extraction allowed for
the organic layer to be easily removed from the aqueous layer due to the differences in density
between camphor and methylene chloride. The anhydrous sodium sulfate was used to dry any
remaining water in the sample. Some sources of error include not evaporating all of the
methylene chloride, adding too much sodium hypochlorite solution at one time, and not refluxing
the solution for long enough. The source of error that most likely occurred is not fully
evaporating the methylene chloride, seeing as though the final yield of the sample was well over
100%. This could have been avoided by allowing the sample to sit over the hot water bath for a
longer amount of time.
